Privacy

What each part of Reins can see, stated precisely enough to be checked against the source.

Last updated 15 August 2026.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, email, or phone number.
  • Your conversations, code, and commands are encrypted between your phone and your Mac. The relay cannot decrypt them.
  • The relay stores nothing on disk and keeps no logs of its own.
  • There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, and no advertising or tracking code in the app.
  • On the same Wi-Fi, nothing touches the relay at all.

What the relay can see

The app and Bridle perform a Noise IK handshake with each other and exchange sealed frames. The relay switches those frames between two sockets by circuit number. It has no key material and cannot read the contents.

It does necessarily observe:

Machine identifier A random per-machine ID generated when Bridle is installed. It is not derived from your hardware and is not linked to you.
Machine name The display name Bridle reports, so the app can show you which Mac you are about to connect to. This is in the clear, and it defaults to your computer's name — edit machineName in ~/.reins/bridle.json if that name says more about you than you would like.
Bridle version Reported at registration.
Who is connected to whom That some phone has an open circuit to some machine, and when it opened.
Traffic volume and timing Unavoidable for anything that forwards bytes. Enough to tell that you are busy, not what you are doing.
Network address Your IP address, as with any server you connect to.

It never sees: your prompts, the model's replies, file contents, diffs, command output, your Harness's address, your API keys, or your file paths.

During pairing

While a pairing code is outstanding — at most 15 minutes, and usually a few seconds — the relay holds the pairing bundle so the app can fetch it with the short code. That bundle contains your machine's name, its public key, its local network addresses, and a one-time token. It is dropped as soon as the code is claimed or expires.

What the relay stores

Nothing on disk. Everything above lives in the relay process's memory and is gone when the process restarts. There is no database, no log file, and no backup.

Two consequences, one good and one worth knowing: we could not hand over your history because we do not have it; and a relay restart drops every open connection, after which the apps reconnect on their own.

Cloudflare

reins.novabox.ai is served through Cloudflare, which terminates TLS and forwards traffic to the relay. Cloudflare therefore sits on the network path and can observe the same metadata the relay can — your IP address, timing, and volume — subject to its own privacy policy. It cannot read your traffic either: the payload it forwards is already sealed between your phone and your Mac.

What stays on your devices

On the Mac

Bridle keeps its identity key and its list of paired phones under ~/.reins. Your conversations stay wherever the DeepSeek Harness already keeps them; Bridle does not copy them anywhere.

On the phone

Less than you might expect. The app keeps its identity key in the iOS Keychain, and four small things in its own sandboxed storage: the list of paired Macs, the name this iPhone reports, which Mac it was last on, and the model new conversations should start with.

Your conversations are not stored on the phone at all. The session list and every message are fetched from your Mac when you open the app and held only in memory, so closing it discards them. That is a consequence of the Mac being the only source of truth rather than a privacy feature we are claiming credit for — but the effect is the same, and it is worth knowing that a phone with Reins on it is not carrying a copy of your work.

Unpairing a Mac in the app's settings is one-sided: the phone forgets that machine, but the Mac still lists the phone until you run bridle revoke there. We could have made the app ask the Mac to forget it, and chose not to — a phone that wants to be forgotten is exactly the phone whose word should not be taken for it. Run bridle devices on the Mac to see what it still trusts.

Notifications

Push notifications are not implemented. When they are, they will require sending an Apple device token to the relay and associating it with a machine, and the notification body will not contain your content. This page will be updated before that ships, not after.

Children

Reins is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

Material changes will be reflected here with a new date at the top. Because the relay keeps no record of who uses it, there is no mailing list to notify — checking this page is the only mechanism, which is a real limitation of collecting nothing.

Checking any of this

The claims above are structural, so they can be verified rather than trusted. The relay's entire state is in relay/src/registry.ts and relay/src/offers.ts, and a test named the relay only ever sees ciphertext asserts the central claim on every run.
